Knife skills basics

How to hold a knife, keep it sharp, and cut onions, herbs and vegetables safely and evenly — the foundation of faster, better cooking.

The only knife you need to start

A single 20 cm (8 inch) chef's knife handles almost everything: chopping, slicing, dicing and mincing. Add a small paring knife for peeling and detail work, and a serrated knife for bread and tomatoes. Cheap knives are fine to learn with; a sharp cheap knife beats a blunt expensive one.

How to hold a knife

Grip the handle with your whole hand, and pinch the blade just above the handle between your thumb and index finger. This «pinch grip» gives you control and stops the knife from wobbling. Your other hand forms a «claw»: fingertips curled under, knuckles guiding the blade, thumb tucked safely behind.

Keeping your knife sharp

A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one, because it cuts cleanly instead of slipping. Hone the edge with a steel rod before each use, and have the knife professionally sharpened once or twice a year. Test sharpness on a tomato: a sharp knife slices the skin with almost no pressure.

Cutting an onion

  1. Cut the onion in half from root to tip, and peel off the skin.
  2. Place the flat side down and make horizontal cuts toward the root, without cutting through it.
  3. Make vertical cuts, again stopping just before the root.
  4. Slice across to make an even dice. The root holds everything together until the end.

Chopping herbs

Gather the leaves into a tight pile, hold them down with your claw hand, and rock the knife through them. For a fine chop, keep the tip of the knife on the board and rock the blade up and down. Do not press and twist — that bruises the herbs and turns them dark.

Dicing vegetables evenly

Even pieces cook evenly. Cut the vegetable into flat slices, stack the slices into sticks, then cut the sticks across into cubes. Take your time: speed comes with practice, and even a slow cook with good knife skills finishes dinner faster than a fast cook who has to fish out burnt pieces.

Safety rules

  • Always cut away from your body.
  • Never catch a falling knife — step back and let it drop.
  • Keep the blade pointing away from you when carrying it.
  • Wash and dry knives by hand, and store them in a block or on a magnetic strip — never loose in a drawer.
