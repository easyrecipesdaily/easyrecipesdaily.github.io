Meal prep for beginners

Cook once, eat all week: a simple Sunday routine that saves time, money and weeknight stress — without eating the same thing every day.

What meal prep really is

Meal prep is not cooking seven identical boxes of plain chicken and rice. It is preparing a few versatile building blocks — a grain, a protein, a sauce and some vegetables — so that assembling dinner takes five minutes instead of forty.

The Sunday routine

  1. Plan (10 minutes). Choose 3 dinners for the week and check what you already have.
  2. Shop once. Buy everything on one list, using our Meal Planner to build it.
  3. Cook the bases (60–90 minutes). Cook a big pot of rice, roast a tray of vegetables, grill or bake the week's protein, and make one sauce.
  4. Pack and label. Store everything in separate containers with the date. Keep components separate so nothing goes soggy.

What preps well — and what does not

Preps wellDoes not prep well
Rice, pasta, grainsSalad leaves (wilt)
Roast chicken, beef, chickpeasFried food (goes soft)
Roasted vegetablesAvocado (browns)
Soups, stews, curriesCrisp toppings (add fresh)
Sauces and dressingsSoft cheese in hot dishes

Mix and match, don't repeat

The same roast chicken becomes three different dinners: chicken and rice bowls on Monday, chicken wraps on Wednesday, and chicken soup on Friday. One batch of rice serves as a side, a stir-fry base and a fried-rice dinner. Variety comes from sauces and toppings, not from cooking five different meals.

Storage basics

Cool food within two hours of cooking, store it in shallow containers, and keep it in the fridge for 3–4 days or the freezer for up to 3 months. Label every container with the dish and the date. Reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

Start small

Your first week, prep just two dinners and one lunch. Once the routine feels easy, add a third dinner. The goal is less stress, not a perfect Instagram fridge.
