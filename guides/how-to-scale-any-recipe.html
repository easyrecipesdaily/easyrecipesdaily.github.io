How to scale any recipe up or down

Doubling a soup is easy; doubling a cake is not. Learn what scales cleanly, what needs adjusting, and how to avoid the classic mistakes.

The basic rule

Multiply every ingredient by the same factor: new servings divided by original servings. A recipe for 4 that you want to serve to 8 uses a factor of 2; a recipe for 6 that you want to serve to 3 uses a factor of 0.5. Our Recipe Scaler does this maths for you.

What scales cleanly

  • Soups, stews and curries — double or halve everything, including the liquid. Cooking time grows only slightly.
  • Pasta and rice dishes — scale the dry ingredients and the sauce in proportion.
  • Roasts and grilled meat — scale the weight of meat per person (about 150–200 g per adult) rather than the recipe.

What does not scale cleanly

Baking is the exception. Yeast, baking powder and baking soda should not be doubled exactly — use about 1.5 times when doubling, and slightly less than half when halving. Salt and strong spices are the same: scale them by about three-quarters of the factor, then taste and adjust. Eggs cannot be halved easily; beat an egg and use half by weight (about 25 g) when you need half an egg.

Cooking time and pan size

Doubling a recipe does not double the cooking time. A doubled cake may need only 10–15 minutes more, not twice as long. Use a bigger pan instead of a deeper one: a thin layer cooks evenly, a deep one burns outside and stays raw inside. For very large batches, make two separate batches instead of one giant one.

Halving a recipe

Halving works well for most dishes, but watch the pan: a small amount of food in a large pan dries out and burns. Use a smaller pan and check the food a few minutes early. Cooking times shrink by roughly a quarter.

The golden rule

Scale by weight, taste as you go, and write down what worked. After two or three attempts you will know exactly how your favourite recipes behave at any size.
